## Decisions

- 2026-10-27 · a primitive the encounter composes with (play/*, mind/*, tools/*, world) works for any rulebook and is never rulebook-built; the dnd5e encounter is rulebook-specific and may know its rulebook, so the protected seam is primitive ↔ rulebook, not encounter ↔ rulebook (the operator calls this "an actual law") · options: primitives built for one rulebook, primitives that serve any rulebook · because: play/* and world are the model to copy, one concern each and deliberately ignorant of everything else ([rpg-project CLAUDE.md, the lens](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/blob/main/CLAUDE.md#the-lens--read-this-before-anything-else)) · [rpg-project#694](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-27 · an encounter may be built outside the rulebooks, for example a clock and intel management layer, and the dnd5e encounter may use it; the operator calls that "not only fine but a good way forward" · options: every encounter lives inside a rulebook, a generic encounter beneath the dnd5e one · because: a new complex thing first looks for the tool upgrade that makes it composable, pluggable and extensible · [rpg-project#694](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · mind may speak in tool types: mind being spatially aware enhances it, tools/selectables is the next tool it draws on when a grab bag or loot table wants it, and "holds no board" means mind keeps no map state and reads no geometry, carrying a cell the caller gave it and handing it back · options: mind taking cells as opaque values in its own type · because: tools are primitives, not rulebooks, so the law that primitives are never rulebook-built does not forbid the import, and a second hex coordinate type inside mind would be two implementations of one thing (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-project#694, play leaves](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462010434) · upheld: [rpg-toolkit#1819](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/1819) finding 2 accepted the import · routed from `mind/`, which has no map yet
- 2026-10-28 · a mechanism that could become a primitive may live inside mind or another composition first, and that is the preferred order: letting it grow where it is used defines the use case for the tool before the tool is built, and extraction follows the defined use case · options: building the primitive ahead of its use case · because: a use case brings the mechanism; a primitive built first guesses at its contract; the mind primitives were born this way (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-project#694, play leaves](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462010434) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · world and mind/* are primitives with their own numbered contracts, bound by never-rulebook-built and never-publishes; they may compose other primitives and may take a context.Context; a true leaf sits at the very edge and these sit one layer in; CLAUDE.md's layering section gains that sentence · options: holding world and mind to the leaf promise and stripping their context · because: the law that matters for a primitive is that it serves any rulebook, and the leaf promise (core only, no context) was written for four small leaves whose callers never cancel (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-project#694, play leaves](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462010434) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · not every decision is an ADR: an ADR is for the big moments and the seam boundaries, an architecture decision that weighed approaches, which makes sense until it does not and is then updated, never silently replaced; ordinary rulings are decisions.md lines; a design doc that made an architecture or seam decision is locatable and tracked (its ADR names it, docs/adr/DECISIONS.md indexes it, the level's seam map carries a decided-by line per seam); the composable encounter is backfilled as ADR-0048 and the Pose-to-Pause change is an update note on ADR-0038 · options: treating every design table as an ADR; a parallel toolkit series that re-writes cross-repo designs · because: ADRs carry the weighing, and the weighing is what a later question needs; a ledger line carries the decision (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-toolkit#2142](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/issues/2142) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-27 · every Go module appears in exactly one knowledge map, the nearest one above it: a layer with its own map is one box at the root and its modules are drawn only there; examples/* are drawn at the root in their own never-tagged group; a module node is a rectangle whose label begins with its path, and the drift check reads nodes, not arrows · options: one hand-drawn repo diagram; drawing a module in two maps; excluding examples/* silently · because: composes, never redraws is otherwise unenforced, and the one existing diagram had drifted (7 of 29 modules missing, 2 dead) within two months · [S1 run record](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2136) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · a new kind of thing is formed in its own module under examples/ before it launches, with its tests and benchmarks, importing the real seams it will bind to by pinned version rather than toy stand-ins; the PR that lands it in its home deletes the example and says in knowledge what moved where · options: forming inside the destination module (every shape change is a pin-coupled PR); keeping the example as documentation after launch · because: play and world were both formed in examples/ before they launched (the operator, 2026-10-28), and the three examples still in the tree after mind and world launched (examples/behavior, examples/perception, examples/world, imported by nothing outside examples/) are how forming places become the next dead attempt · first deliberate use, composable conditions: [rpg-project#703](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/703), [rpg-toolkit#2144](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2144) · upheld: play/* and world/ (testimony; their PRs are not linked yet)
- 2026-10-28 · two tools, each named for the sum of its responsibilities: machine is a state machine over a bag that fires configured events on transition (states, transitions, trackers, guards, bag, blob, Validate) and knows no chain; effects is the composer that runs a machine from a recipe and attaches the current state's outputs to the chains. They form together in one example module, examples/effects, with effects/machine a package inside it, tests their only customer and a Rage-shaped test vocabulary; when conditions first consumes them the module is promoted to its home as effects with effects/machine still a package, and machine becomes its own module only when something outside effects pulls on it. Neither imports a rulebook (core and events only); the rulebook plugs in a vocabulary (fact readers over its frame, beats over its typed topics, output kinds over its chains, named amounts), conditions supplies it and the descriptor glue, and the tool validates every recipe against the vocabulary it was given (composable conditions ruling 6 of 6, E9's fourth cut) · options: growing the machine inside rulebooks/dnd5e/conditions and lifting it to a neutral home when a second puller arrives (E9's first cut); a neutral module formed in examples/ with a toy game (second cut); a machine package inside the dnd5e root module with a seam test and no examples/ detour (third cut, 'we do not need a toy, let's make it our current use case'); machine alone in examples with the composer in conditions; the name conditionmachine · because: 'why would the example need the rulebook? this is the tool the rulebook would use right?'; 'if it is a state machine that fires configured events out on transition that are used by a composer then machine makes sense … alternatively we could shoot for making the effects package in example and have a path to effects/machine' (the operator, 2026-10-28); generic tools, not game rules, is the toolkit's first law · [rpg-toolkit#2144](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2144) (E2, E9, S1, S2), [rpg-project#703](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/703) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · a condition is a state machine (explicit states with outputs, transitions on beats under guards, trackers feeding one JSON bag), and guards and amounts are a closed vocabulary that is the frame's own fact list (fact/flag/round/class-level leaves, a short op list, named amounts), validated when the registry loads a recipe; no expression language crosses the seam between the machine and the rulebook, and nothing is brought in or copied from earlier work elsewhere · options: typed Go atoms with closures and generics (the first draft, read as 'very code centric'); a machine with JSONata guards over a JSON projection ('I def do not want to bring in jsonata at the seams anyway'); statekit from the operator's earlier work (a state bag with expressions, not a machine; 'I never want to take, I always want to build it new') · because: the dead attempts failed on invented filter strings and a home-made interpreter; the frame is the one place a fact is defined and already answers applies / does-not / depends; a recipe is data from the first slice, which is what the studio builds (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-toolkit#2144](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2144) (E2, E3, E11), [rpg-project#703](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/703) · upheld: none yet

## Findings

- 2026-10-27 · day-one drift of the old architecture diagram: 24 nodes for 29 modules, 7 missing (npc, world, mind/behavior, play/interrupt, the 3 examples) and 2 naming deleted modules (mechanics/conditions, mechanics/spells); top-level behavior/ and spawn/ each hold one doc.go, no go.mod and no code; items, mechanics/features and mechanics/proficiency have no importer among the 29 modules · [S1 run record](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2136)
- 2026-10-27 · a change under scripts/ or a workflow makes ci-optimized run every module's lint and tests, about ten minutes, so a docs PR that adds a script pays the full suite · [S1 run record](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2136)
- 2026-10-28 · two earlier attempts to compose conditions from effects are dead: mechanics/effects (ComposedCondition with Temporary, Dice, Stackable and Saves behaviors over string-named events and wall-clock durations; imported only by mechanics/features and mechanics/proficiency, which have no importer themselves) and rulebooks/dnd5e/effects (an Effect struct carrying bonuses as strings such as '+1d4' and a Rage whose damage bonus is a constant commented 'Varies by barbarian level'; no importer). Both modeled filters as strings a generic interpreter had to know, and the first filter the strings could not express sent the rule back to hand-written code. What exists instead: eleven conditions carry a free-standing rule value that reads the typed frame and answers applies, does not apply or depends, with a near-identical fold landing the answer on its chain; that is the vocabulary a decomposition names, not new strings · [mechanics/effects/composed_condition.go](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/dd2ccc8b9a0fc0666194c544a2a6c89671dca12f/mechanics/effects/composed_condition.go), [rulebooks/dnd5e/effects/types.go](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/dd2ccc8b9a0fc0666194c544a2a6c89671dca12f/rulebooks/dnd5e/effects/types.go#L84)
