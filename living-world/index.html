The engine today

The Living World

A world is more than the fight the party is in. Patrols have somewhere to go. A sound can give someone a reason to leave. What one person learns need not become knowledge for everyone.

The ambition is still a world shaped by its players. The toolkit now gives us working pieces for time, perception, knowledge, behavior and interactions—and the game is where we keep testing how they fit.

Clock / opportunity

When can something act?

In free roam, player activity advances world time. A fight uses turn-based scheduling. The clock gives each mode an explicit place instead of making combat the only way the game can move.

NPCs can be due for work or respond to relevant inputs. An inactive creature need not repeatedly choose to do nothing.

Player-driven world time—not a promise of an unattended, wall-clock simulation.

Intel / holdings

Who knows what?

Intel stores what an observer holds about a subject. That information can be current, remembered, or wrong; the store does not consult world truth to correct it.

A topic distinguishes different kinds of knowledge about the same subject, so one holding need not overwrite another.

Observer + subject + topic. Knowledge belongs to a perspective.

Perception / evidence

What reached this observer?

Sight, hearing and reports supply different information. Seeing a speaker is not the same as catching their words. Hearing an unseen voice need not reveal who made it.

The encounter supplies what the senses delivered. The perception layer manages that testimony over intel, without taking over the world’s geometry or deciding what to do next.

The engine knows the scene. A creature acts from its own information.

Mind machine / state

What is it listening for?

A behavior has a current state. That state describes the inputs it listens for, the conditions it checks, and how it changes when something relevant arrives.

A posted guard, an active investigation and a sleeping inhabitant can respond to different inputs. The machine keeps progress; it does not own a map, a clock or the rules of movement.

State changes what matters. The surrounding game supplies the facts.

Behavior / composition

Which job gets this opportunity?

An NPC can carry several behaviors with separate progress. The composer applies authored precedence, then selects from the chosen behavior’s available actions.

Investigation can take priority without advancing or resetting a pending patrol. Two guards can reuse a definition without sharing their mutable state.

Choosing work is not performing it. Completion comes back from the encounter.

Resolution / interaction

How does the work happen?

An attack, a saving throw and a player decision need different steps. Resolution drives interaction machines, assembles participant behavior on its bus, and returns results and changed data.

A supported player-choice boundary can preserve completed work while waiting for an answer. State owners still apply their changes; the host saves the returned data.

These interaction-step machines are distinct from mind’s listening state machines.

Encounter / composition

Who connects the pieces?

The encounter brings the board, clocks, perception, behavior and record together. It supplies the context an interaction needs, executes selected movement, and tells the owning behavior when that work actually finishes.

Its rules come from supplied rulebook capabilities rather than being hidden inside the reusable leaves. A patrol’s continuation can therefore be tested through real movement, not just a proposed action.

The composition owns the wiring. The pieces do not need to know one another.

Record / retained events

What happened—and who gets to see it?

The record retains an ordered account of events with their declared audiences. Two viewers can receive different parts of the same sequence without the log inventing a second version of what happened.

The composition supplies the event and its audience; the record stores and queries them. Streaming to clients and durable storage belong to the host.

Intel is what an observer holds. The record is the retained account of events.

From pieces to play

A response has consequences

The encounter connects these boundaries. It supplies lawful inputs, offers opportunities to act, executes the chosen work, and records what happened. New observations can then change what someone knows or does next.

SITUATIONSomething happens.
PERSPECTIVESomeone learns about it.
RESPONSEA behavior has work to propose.
CONSEQUENCEAn action changes the situation.

This is a conceptual cycle, not an extra action granted by every event. Learning something and being allowed to act are separate. Moving toward a destination and actually arriving are separate too.
