# Decisions — rulebooks/dnd5e

One line per entry, dated and linked. Ingest is the writer; entries arrive through its reviewed PR.
A decision reads `- <date> · <the words> · options: <the options it closed> · because: <why> · <link> · upheld: <links, or none yet>`;
a finding, friction or open line reads `- <date> · <the words> · <link>`. A finding that needs more than a line links its case file in `findings/`.
Friction names both entries and never settles them.

## Decisions

- 2026-08-14 · the bus lives in one place, resolution; session and encounter trade in data · options: every rules package attaches to the bus, resolution alone owns it · because: resolution is the moving part and the composer · [ADR-0038](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/c355102dffe40fa6aa3852d13708ec21c055172d/docs/adr/0038-resolution-owns-the-bus.md) · upheld: none linked yet; reaffirmed by the operator 2026-10-27 ([rpg-project#694](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694))
- 2026-10-27 · "data only" names what happens at the seams of the resolution machine: everything that crosses a seam is data, and the extensions the machine composes with are data, so each extension is trivially unit-testable; the composable data-only pieces hold no randomness. It is a property of the seams and the extensions, not a claim that the encounter never rolls; where else the pattern works is a question, not a rule · options: data only as a property of the whole encounter, data only as a property of the seams and extensions · because: data at every seam is what makes that section work and each extension testable alone · [rpg-project#694](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-27 · where a die rolls follows what the roll is for: a roll that resolves an interaction (attack, check, save, death save, activation) rolls in resolution, which takes a required roller on every Input; a roll that decides what a creature does or how the world orders itself (a behavior table pick, a search heading, an answer to a spoken line, initiative) rolls in the encounter through the driver. The host supplies both rollers at construction and each is refused by name when absent; the roller stays in the encounter (the operator), and a seeded roller per encounter, so a fight cannot be cheesed by replaying it, needs no new seam because the host supplies it through the existing capability ([encounter/capabilities.go:83](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/c355102dffe40fa6aa3852d13708ec21c055172d/rulebooks/dnd5e/encounter/capabilities.go#L83), [resolution/check.go:55](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/c355102dffe40fa6aa3852d13708ec21c055172d/rulebooks/dnd5e/resolution/check.go#L55)) · options: every roll in resolution, rolls split by what they are for · because: a decision is the driver's, not an interaction · [rpg-project#694](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-27 · who decides what a viewer may see is postponed on purpose. Pre-playtest, everyone sees everything; in-game information and stories are shared across the party so all see the same thing; debug detail travels on its own RPC at its own level of detail. The range held open runs from RAW (you know what you saw or what a teammate literally told you; a trap described to you is never shown to you) to all see everything · options: build a visibility mechanism now, postpone it until a use case brings it · because: no mechanism is built for the range until a use case brings it · [rpg-project#694](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-08-24 · the top-level encounter/ module was the early prototype, given up on purpose when the composable format arrived; the encounter moved under rulebooks/dnd5e once it was clear it could live outside that release tree, so a design that reaches for the prototype's shape reaches for something abandoned on purpose, not something lost · options: keep the prototype beside the composable encounter, retire it · because: the composable format replaced it · [rpg-toolkit#1220](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/1220) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · the encounter owns the audience POLICY and session owns the ROUTING: both visibility styles are wanted (what-you-see-is-what-you-get, and party-shared checks and trap knowledge as Baldur's Gate 3 does), the toggle between them is encounter configuration per encounter or per player with discoveryAudience as its model, audienceFor is that configuration, and session is the door on the way out where delivery is adjusted; event-projection R1 and event-stream R35 are amended to read delivery in the session projection, audience in the encounter, and rpg-project#658 resolves in the encounter's favour · options: the session projection as the policy home; leaving the three records as friction · because: session was chosen first because delivery is likely to be adjusted and the exit door is where that happens, a reason that holds for routing and never held for policy; losing that because is what let the toggle land in the wrong layer (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-project#694, two questions](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462060901) · upheld: none yet (the stopped session filter [rpg-toolkit#2108](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2108) predates the ruling)
- 2026-10-28 · the encounter answers its own Witness capability from sightReach, and the session implementation (witnessSeam: distance plus line of sight over Sight ranges, blind to sight areas and sneak suppression) goes, with a test that a sneaking character is unseen by both paths · options: keeping two sight answers as an accepted divergence · because: the encounter owns audience policy and one sight answer lives in one place; two implementations of visibility that can disagree is the hazard rpg-toolkit#940 named on 2026-08-13; had the reason for each placement been written down, the second implementation would have read as a leak when it was built (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-project#694, two questions](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462060901) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · the host seam (session) may hold and route play leaf state as wiring, the custody of open interrupt windows across verbs and reads of the record for the record stream, and never interprets a payload; whether interrupt's custody should sit under the encounter is an open question with no solid case today, and session's play/intel require is used by no non-test file · options: filing interrupt's custody as a gap for the encounter now · because: a window outlives one load-act-save call, and persistence across calls is what the seam exists to absorb so the composition does not have to; interrupt's own design is custody, not execution (the operator, 2026-10-28); the rulebook CLAUDE.md line naming interrupt among the leaves the encounter couriers is corrected · [rpg-project#694, play leaves](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462010434) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · a debit of a declared price is wiring, not a rule: session pays what the rulebook priced (movement per step, a spoken line's price) and plays a walk step by step, since a move has nothing to resolve except interrupt-shaped data that comes back and an interrupt can be waived or sent to the client from session; what must not live in session is the game number, so the literal 5 in move.go becomes the rulebook's encounter.FeetPerCell as social.go already borrows the appeal price by name · options: moving movement and speech debits behind resolution's door · because: paying a declared price is the seam's job, the same as charging the door's cost; routing every movement step through the interaction machine would be the band-aid (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-toolkit#2139](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/issues/2139) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · rollers are required everywhere: passing a mock roller or a real roller is deliberate, and a roller never arrives as a side effect of a missing argument; Sneak Attack, Deflect Missiles and Undead Fortitude take the roller the interaction supplied, the dice.NewRoller() fallbacks go, and a rollerless call is refused by name as resolution's door already does · options: leaving the three handlers until each is next touched · because: a silent default is unreproducible dice in a result that looks fine (rpg-toolkit#1033), and a replayable run from a seeded roller diverges at every handler with its own entropy (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-toolkit#2141](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/issues/2141) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-28 · the sheets borrow resolution's bus: it is attached at the door for the life of one interaction, their conditions subscribe through it, their chains publish on it, saves and checks take it as an input, and no rules package owns or creates a bus or holds one between interactions; the seam map's must-not-cross line reads that way (amended on rpg-toolkit#2136 at f5ac61a9), and the code is not the drift · options: treating the bus fields in character and monster as the drift · because: ADR-0038 closed a bus the rules packages OWN, and that ruling came when duplicate buses existed; borrowing one bus is the mechanism it chose; long term this may not hold, so the line carries its date (the operator, 2026-10-28) · [rpg-project#694, resolution and session](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/694#issuecomment-6462028073) · upheld: none yet
- 2026-10-11 · a condition is one thing that listens to chain events and makes modifications, and it is described once beside its type: one ref, one display, one load, one create, its bearings and any attack override, read through the registry; no other table keyed by a condition ref exists, and monster traits are conditions in the same registry · options: the six parallel tables keyed by ref that preceded it · because: six tables drifted and were reconciled at runtime (operator rulings A1–A4 on [rpg-toolkit#1965](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/issues/1965), shipped as dnd5e v0.210.0) · [rpg-toolkit#2014](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2014) · upheld: Sanctuary, Resistance and the Raging descriptors added since all register through the one table (testimony; their PRs are not linked yet)
- 2026-08-27 · a condition's duration anchors to the round the clock stamps on the turn-end event, never to the sheet's own turn counter, and a round that goes backwards ends the condition rather than re-anchoring it · options: ActionEconomy.TurnNumber on the sheet; re-anchoring on a reset · because: the sheet's counter is a local mirror with a documented staleness defect across fights ([rpg-project#253](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/253)), and re-anchoring would hand out a fresh duration and hide the regression combat end exists to prevent ([rpg-project#295](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/295)) · [raging.go onTurnEnd](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/dd2ccc8b9a0fc0666194c544a2a6c89671dca12f/rulebooks/dnd5e/conditions/raging.go#L294), [rpg-toolkit#1266](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/1266) · upheld: Rage's cap, clock-reset net and derived turns-active all decide on RoundActivated and nothing reads the sheet counter (testimony, checked against raging.go)
- 2026-08-27 · the turn a sustained condition is activated on is not checked for its sustain rule; Rage graces its first turn end, and the divergence from RAW is exactly this one branch · options: RAW by the letter, where a barbarian who rages and does nothing else drops it as that turn ends · because: 'i cannot imagine activating rage would end at the end of the turn activated ... this game does not need to follow RAW to the letter' (the operator, 2026-08-27); a bad moment is not an interesting one · [raging.go onTurnEnd](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/blob/dd2ccc8b9a0fc0666194c544a2a6c89671dca12f/rulebooks/dnd5e/conditions/raging.go#L294) · upheld: the activation swing also does not pay for the next turn's check (testimony; no PR linked)
- 2026-10-28 · conditions, and then the conditions spells deliver, are composed of effects grouped under one ref with one state bag and one budget, the effects driving the condition fully; an effect is configuration (what it subscribes to, what must be true as a filter over frame facts, what it does with the condition's state handed in), in three kinds: modifier, tracker, budget; hand-written Go is the form for now and content authoring comes with the encounter studio; cost never sits on a condition, it is the activation's SpendProfile, and features move onto actions.Definition · options: strings and a generic interpreter (dead twice in this tree); keep hand-writing and extract helpers (never yields one data structure per condition) · because: 'they are really a series of effects with a budget'; 'the goal of configuring to subscribe and act on things with the state param filled in is what we want'; 'cost is outside the executing of a condition or spell'; 'no need to move quickly here' (the operator, 2026-10-28); where the machine and composer live is the repo-root line of the same date · [rpg-project#703](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-project/issues/703), design [rpg-toolkit#2144](https://github.com/KirkDiggler/rpg-toolkit/pull/2144) (E1–E10) · upheld: none yet

- 2026-10-28 · three levels: the machine package is code that knows the grammar and no condition; a recipe is the configuration of one condition type, loaded once; an instance is one recipe on one holder, whose bag is filled at create from the applier's parameters and written while it runs.
