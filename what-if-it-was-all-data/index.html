What If It Was All Data?

The table and holdable plushie were pieces of the Living World I wanted to build. Underneath that visible progress was an earlier discovery: breaking up the encounter application changed what we needed from the machinery running it.

01The pieces
Before the table

The breakup changed the question

We had started breaking the encounter application into tools. The new play/* packages gave clocks, knowledge, records and interruptions their own contracts. We could compose an encounter from those pieces rather than make one large application responsible for everything.

That made the old combat machinery’s shape harder to ignore. It still had a little of everything in it. We were starting to find the seams.

02The pressure
The old shape did not fit

Who was running the interaction?

The new encounter composition returned values and needed no event bus. Combat still required one. It published turn events even though the clock already supplied that information.

Attaching behavior was scattered too. Loading a character included its effects; a monster needed several calls. Forgetting one could leave its traits out of the fight without an error. These weren’t just awkward connections. The responsibilities needed clearer homes.

03The question
What if we passed it all in?

The bus wasn’t disappearing

What if characters and monsters were data we passed into resolution? It could assemble their behavior, drive the interaction, and return the result and changed data.

Removing the bus from those pieces didn’t mean removing the bus. Resolution gave it a clear home. Effects could still contribute their behavior; the owners of state still applied their changes.

04The learning
My understanding had to catch up

A place for the next step

Strike was a machine: a sequence of steps, with rules for what came next. It needed an attack roll. A spell might need a saving throw instead, or no roll at all. We could assemble the steps that interaction needed rather than force it through an attack.

That was what excited me. However complicated a spell sounded, I could begin to see where its checks, interruptions and consequences belonged. A contribution could describe follow-up work; the returned result could determine the next step. Resolution would drive it. We hadn’t solved every spell, but we had a place to put the work.

It took me a few updates to understand how those pieces worked together. I remember being on a run, reading my phone in the light rain and trying to get my head around it. I was still thinking about carrying frozen rolls across a boundary in terms of the old approach. We had changed the architecture. I was still learning how to think with it.

05The payoff
The interaction could wait

Spells became more approachable

Interrupts had been a major blocker. Shield was always on my mind: an attack underway, with a reaction that could change whether it landed. Other spells needed saves rather than attacks. They couldn’t all follow one fixed sequence.

The later Bardic Inspiration tests gave us a concrete pause: preserve the attack roll, ask whether to spend the die, then continue without rerolling or repeating completed work.

The definitions and their contributions were easier to test. Spells I had been avoiding became things I could reason about building.

The bus hadn’t disappeared.
We had found the seam that let more things use it.

Evidence trail

The gradual realization, reading my phone on a run in light rain, and Shield as a recurring motivation are my recollections. The exact exchange about frozen rolls has not been recovered. This note looks back from the previous article’s September authoring and holdable-object examples to the August breakup and resolution work; it does not make those later objects the cause of the earlier architecture.

The contemporary resolution discussion records the mismatch between bus-free composition and bus-dependent combat, inconsistent attachment paths, and my proposal to pass participant data into resolution. The accepted boundary gives resolution bus custody while machines trade in steps. State-owner handlers still apply changes; this was not the elimination of executable effects or a claim that every feature had become pure data.

The August action dispatcher initially accepts only attack profiles. The September cast branch interprets save-versus-direct-delivery data and reuses existing machines. A new spell does not necessarily need a new machine.

The Bardic Inspiration test assertions preserve the d20 across spend/keep answers and check that the post-roll chain runs exactly once. That snapshot also refuses suspension inside a requested child machine. It is not proof that every nested reaction or Shield interaction was delivered.
